# Balance log

Every change to `src/engine/tunables.ts` gets a line here saying what changed and why.

- 2026-09-02: Recorded the starting values from DESIGN.md section 4. No changes yet.
- 2026-09-02: Phase 5 baseline, 5,000 matches at seed 1 with the starting values. Three of four targets failed: EV 70% and Muscle 64% against the field, Super 68% against the field, and Daily-only 4% against Hyper-only. Hyper cars made their first advance a mean 1.4 turns after staging, so bench fueling made the cost 5 nearly free. Off-road won 15% and JDM 29%, confirming the heavy-type risk. Median match 13 turns per player. Race winners kept their car 63% of the time, so wear at 10% leaves the swap a real decision.
- 2026-09-02: Fuel cost by tier 1/2/3/5 to 1/2/4/6. The cost step is what moves Daily against Hyper: 1/2/4/7 gave Daily 59% and 1/3/5/8 gave it 77%, both by turning race 1 on whether a fueled Hyper finishes before a Daily car does. Super at 4 and Hyper at 6 put Daily-only at 51% against Hyper-only and the tiers at 3/51/55/40 against the field.
- 2026-09-02: Per-type distance multiplier added as a tunable and set to Off-road 1.2, JDM 1.2, Luxury 1.1, others 1. Off-road 1.3 overshot to 72% once fuel costs rose, since every Off-road car is a cheap tier. At 1.2 the types sit between 49% and 55%. The worked example in DESIGN.md 3.3 now shows the Civic Si at 243 ft.
- 2026-09-02: EV first-advance bonus 100 ft to 75 ft. EV was the top type at 58% to 62% across every sweep; 75 ft brought it to 55% to 59% while keeping it the launch type.
- 2026-09-02: Wear rate kept at 0.1. Sweeps at 0.15 and 0.2 lowered the winner keep rate from 58% to about 50% and pulled Daily-only against Hyper-only down toward 35% without helping the other targets.
- 2026-09-02: Final check, 5,000 matches at seed 1 after tuning, and 6,000-match runs at seeds 2 and 3 during the sweep. All four targets pass: max single-type 55%, max single-tier 55%, Daily-only 51% against Hyper-only, median 13 turns per player. Starters run 60%, 51%, and 65% for the first-named side; their decks are still placeholders until phase 7.
- 2026-09-02: Starter decklists finalized (phase 7). No tunable changed. Final lists keep Street Kings and Exotic Garage as drafted and soften Electric Avenue (fewer launch and sabotage cards, Pit Crew ×3, Sponsor ×3). At 1,000 matches per pairing with the CPU on both sides: Street Kings 57% over Exotic Garage, Exotic Garage 56% over Electric Avenue, Electric Avenue 63% over Street Kings; overall 47%, 50%, 53%.
- 2026-09-03: Collection tunables added (phase 11): 1 pack per match, 2 for beating the CPU, packs of 2 cars and 3 mods, car tier odds 55/30/12/3. - 2026-09-08: Loaner garages split from the intro set (phase 28), no tunable changed. The three prebuilt garages stay exactly as they are and stop being the free grant; a fresh collection now owns six cars and sixteen mods at two copies, 22 of 159 cards instead of 46. The old grant handed over 31 of the 33 mods with 14 already at the three-copy deck limit, so 44% of a pack's mod slots were dead on the first pack and two of the thirteen Ultra Rare cars were free; now none are. Re-check at 5,000 matches, seed 1: every target passes at its previous value, types 56%, tiers 62%, Daily-only against Hyper-only 53%, median 14 turns, and the loaner cycle is unchanged at 56/51/63, since `STARTERS` and the roster did not move. The intro set was tuned by measurement: a first car list built on the 911 Carrera S, WRX STI and Prius ran 25% against the field where the loaners run 43 to 50, because four of its cars sat on the ×1.0 distance multiplier and two were under 240 ft; swapping to the Raptor R, Ioniq 5 N and RX-7 brought it to 46%, and against the loaners it runs 47/46/41. Concentrating the mods into a three-copy spine was tried and dropped: about three points for six dead pack slots. Pack simulator at 2,000 runs, seed 1: every card takes a mean of 720 packs (median 663) against 690 before, the first Ultra Rare 16.5 (median 12) and now genuinely the first, and every mod in the game 38 (median 36).

Display names only; the ids stay `exotic-garage` and `electric-avenue`. Entries above this line use the old names, and the simulator's pairing rows use the new ones from here on. - 2026-09-09: Turn timer tunables added (phase 29), `online.turnLimitMs` 90,000 and `online.disconnectGraceMs` 30,000: a ranked seat has ninety seconds to act, restarted on every accepted action, and a dropped seat stops its clock for thirty seconds in total per turn before it runs anyway. Ninety seconds is long enough for a fuel, mods and advance turn with real card decisions and short enough that a walked-away opponent is not held for long; the grace covers a phone changing network without letting a seat stall by reconnecting on a loop. No effect on match balance, since the CPU always acts and the sim never times out; recorded because they live in the tunables.
- 2026-09-09: Scrapping and buying added (phase 30) with two tunables, `collection.scrapValue` 1/2/5/12 and `collection.cardPrice` 40/80/200/500 by tier, a mod grading as Common or, when rare, as Rare. The case, measured over 200 simulated collections from the intro set: a pack holds a card the player does not own 95% of the time over the first twenty-five, 46% by the hundredth and 5% past the two hundredth, while the share of held cards that no deck can use reaches 45% by pack 50, 65% by pack 100 and 93% by pack 600. Surplus is overwhelmingly cheap — after 400 packs a collection holds about 1,076 spare common mods and 404 spare Dailies against 13 spare Hypers — so the exchange has to let cheap copies buy dear cards. Rates were picked by measurement, spending credits on the cheapest missing card, which is the most efficient possible play and so an upper bound on the effect: packs to own every card fall from a mean of 710 without conversion to 323 at 20/40/100/250, 366 at 30/60/150/350, and 408 at the 40/80/200/500 shipped. The last keeps the collection a long road while making the endgame answerable, where packs alone had stopped giving anything. No match tunable changed and the four targets are untouched, since this is collection-side only.
- 2026-09-09: The random number generator was widened from mulberry32, whose state is one 32-bit word, to xoshiro128** over four (phase 37, backlog S5). No tunable changed; recorded here because every match's random stream changed with it, so figures taken before this line came from a different sequence. Balance is unchanged, checked at 40,000 matches, seed 1, on both generators: max single type 57% before and 56% after, max single tier 65% and 64%, Daily-only against Hyper-only 50% both, median 14 turns both. Worth knowing for anyone reading a target failure: at the usual 5,000 matches the type and tier cells hold about 250 games each, which is roughly plus or minus six points, and on the new stream seed 1 fails the type cap at 64%, seeds 2 and 3 fail the tier cap at 66%, and seed 4 passes everything. The targets are sound; the 5,000-match run is too small to gate on at the caps, which is backlog G17.
- 2026-09-09: Two tunables added for the creation limit (phase 38, backlog S6), `online.creationsPerWindow` 5 and `online.creationWindowMs` one hour. The numbers are the ones the worker already used; they move here because the limit itself moved to the account directory, which reads the tunables, and because a number that decides who can play should not be a literal in the adapter. No effect on match balance.
- 2026-09-09: The simulator's default run went from 5,000 matches to 20,000 and a fifth target was added, that the first player wins between 47% and 55% (phase 42, backlog G17 and G10). No tunable changed. At 5,000 each type and tier cell held 250 to 300 games, about six points either way, so a cap could fail on one seed and pass on the next; at 20,000 the type, Daily-against-Hyper, length and first-player figures are steady across seeds 1, 2 and 3 (types 59/56/59, Daily 48/50/48, median 14 throughout, first player 51/51/52). **The tier cap is not steady, and that is a real finding rather than noise**: 65%, 66% and 65% on those three seeds, so the Rare tier sits at the cap and passes or fails by the seed. The balance log has called Rare the closest to its cap since 2026-09-03; with four times the samples it is at it. Left as it is and filed as backlog G18, because moving a tier is a game decision rather than something to slip into a sweep.
- 2026-09-09: A sixth target added, that no single-tier garage wins less than 20% against the field (phase 43, backlog G5 and G18). No tunable changed. **It does not pass**, and that is the point of adding it: at 20,000 matches, seed 1, the tiers run Common 3%, Uncommon 50%, Rare 65%, Ultra Rare 39%. The four original caps only ever stopped a tier running away with it, so a band nobody can win with was invisible to them, and `collection.carTierOdds` puts 55% of every pack's car slots in the Common band -- most of what a player opens is a tier that loses. The shape is not the fuel-cost step working as intended: Common against Hyper is 48%, inside its target, because a Hyper garage is slow to fuel; Common loses to the middle, 10% against Uncommon and 6% against Rare, which are fast and still cheap. Rare at 65% and Ultra Rare at 39% are the same curve seen from the other end, so G5 and G18 are one finding. Left untuned deliberately: moving `fuelCostByTier` or the type multipliers to lift the floor changes how every match feels and wants sweeps and an owner's judgement, not a line in a backlog sweep. The measurement is now permanent, so the next attempt starts from a number that fails rather than from a gap nothing reported.

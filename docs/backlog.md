# Backlog

Everything open: defects, risks, gaps, and features not yet built. One line each, worst first.

**How to use it.** Pick from the top. When you take something on, set its state to `doing`; when it lands, set `done` and add the phase and commit, the way `BUILD_PLAN.md` does for phases. Anything new goes in with the next free id in its letter.

**Ids never change or get reused.** `1`–`23` are the original feature items and keep the numbers `DESIGN.md` and past commits refer to. Findings from the 2026-09-09 audit take a letter: **S** security, **Q** code and tooling, **G** gameplay and balance, **A** accessibility, **D** docs, **U** interface rules, **P** performance.

**Kinds** are 🐛 `bug`, ⚠️ `risk`, 🧩 `gap`, ✨ `feat` and ⚡ `perf`. A `perf` row carries its own category as the first words of the item — **Load** is what ships before the game starts, **Render** is what an interaction costs, **Assets** is images and audio, **Storage** is what is written and read.

**Sev** is how much it matters: for a defect, how bad; for a feature, how many players feel it and how often — 🔴 `high`, 🟠 `med`, 🟢 `low`. A superscript marks a row with a note below.

The badge on each row repeats its kind and severity, so the table can be scanned down a column without reading the words. A row with no severity is one where the question does not apply.

A count is only as good as the files it covers: the one that closed this ran over every tracked file and read each G18 left in context.
66. Q54's lesson: a list of what a service accepts is no evidence that anything sends it. Fifteen names went into the allow list when the stats were built and only eight were ever wired to a moment in the game, and nothing compared the two, so the finished column printed zero from the first day. A zero is the most convincing wrong answer a report can give, because it looks like a measurement; this one surfaced only when a review asked what real play looked like and the numbers did not add up. The list now has a test beside it that fails when a name has no sender.
67. The type lab's method, and what its first reading said. A type's result against the field mixes three things: its rules and cars, which tiers its cars sit in, and how the CPU plays it. The lab pulls them apart with two extra readings, one with the tiers held equal and one with the Pro CPU. Both sides get the same tiers, rather than the type facing a full random garage, so 50% there means the type makes no difference; the tiers come only from those every type has cars in, which leaves Hyper out, since JDM and Off-road have none. The verdict is the reading with a margin rather than the whole range inside the band, because with six types one range misses its true value about a quarter of the time. The first reading: Off-road is by far the strongest type for its tier, 65% with the tiers held equal, and its cheap tiers hide it; Sports is strong only through its tiers and through the Street CPU reading every coin flip as tails except the one the Sports rule makes heads, and drops 9 points under Pro; EV's cars below Hyper are the weakest for their tier. The six types average 51.7%, not 50, so the top edge binds. A likely cause, not yet checked: a single-type garage can always play its type-locked Boost, where a random one can play it only while a car of that type is staged.
68. G21's lesson: a stand-in that is exact for one case can be wrong for the rest in a way that picks sides. A standing-start advance is the right number for a car's first advance and the wrong one for its race, and the error was not noise: it favoured every type whose bonus comes at the start and hid every one whose bonus comes later, so the CPU's own play leaned the type numbers it was used to measure. The fix also showed what a floor sitting on its limit costs: Street getting better read as Pro getting worse, and only the same pairing run on five seeds, before and after, showed the drop was real and that the old floor had cleared on two of them.
69. What G22 is and is not. The type band is measured with the Street CPU on both sides, since Street is what most matches are played against, and Street's one cautious habit, reading every coin flip as tails, happens to favour one type: the Sports identity is the only thing that makes a flip certain. Tuning a type to a CPU habit changes the rules for every player to suit one opponent's blind spot, and the Pro reading, which plays coin flips the way a person does, puts Sports a little under average, not over. So the type stays at 1 and the habit is the question. Off-road's twelve-point gap runs the other way and is not explained yet; a first guess, unchecked, is that Pro plays Traction sabotage by the turns it would save and Off-road ignores all of it.
70. Note 69 was wrong about the cause, and how that was found. It rested on a mechanism that fitted, the Street CPU never playing coin-flip cards except when the Sports rule makes heads certain, and on a change that moved Sports the right way; neither measured the cause. Giving Street each of Pro's four skills on its own, and taking each from Pro, did: reading flips at their odds moved Sports about 2 points and Pro's lead over Street under 1, while staging by the turns a car needs to finish moved Sports 7, Off-road 12 and nearly all of Pro's lead, which falls below even without it. Note 69's Off-road guess, Traction sabotage, was wrong too: holding stalls moved it nothing. One change at a time is what separates a cause from a story that fits.
